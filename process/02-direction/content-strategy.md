# Content strategy

Audience: recruiters and hiring managers, reached through a career fair (NFC tap or QR), a LinkedIn click, or a look before an interview.

## The 15-second story

1. Juan Pablo Hoyos Castedo, finance and financial analytics at the Walton College, graduating December 2026.
2. He wants analyst work: financial or data. He is also open to sales and consulting.
3. Proof: a live Python portfolio-analytics app, a Sam's Club redesign that was a Demo Day finalist, and tutoring that helped the football program to its best GPA on record.
4. Reach him: email, LinkedIn, résumé, all one tap away.

## The 60-second story

He sits between business and technology. He builds the analysis in Python (risk-adjusted returns, Markowitz optimization, a deployed Streamlit app), he shapes it into something a customer will use (a Sam's Club service-desk redesign projected to cut wait times 87.5%), and he explains it to people who need it (1000–4000-level business courses and Programming Fundamentals for student-athletes, plus study halls for the football and baseball programs). Three internships in Santa Cruz, Bolivia gave him the customer side: 500-plus customers studied and segmented, 80 recovered, a SaaS platform sold to 25 veterinary centers and taught to 20 owners who had never used software.

## Page order, and why

1. **Spec-sheet hero.** Name, one-line positioning, then four key-value rows (role, base, status, stack) and three calls to action. This answers the 15-second questions before any scrolling.
2. **Case files.** Four, ordered by what a recruiter cares about, not by date.
3. **Experience.** The full résumé timeline, compact, so nothing is hidden.
4. **Skills.** Grouped, and each group points at the case file that proves it. No percentage bars.
5. **How this site was built.** Baseline, tool comparison, final. It turns the assignment's process into a selling point: I can direct AI tools, not just use them.
6. **Contact.** Email, LinkedIn, GitHub, résumé, links page.

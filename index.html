CF-02 Shipped Spring 2026

Portfolio Tracker App

Financial Data Analytics II (FINN 43303) · built for the course, deployed and public

Problem
Comparing portfolios on return alone hides the risk taken to earn it.
What I did
Built and deployed a Streamlit app in Python: Sharpe and Sortino ratios, rolling volatility and drawdowns, correlation and covariance matrices, and Markowitz optimization for the global minimum-variance (GMV) and tangency portfolios along the efficient frontier, with per-asset risk contribution, a weight-slider builder and estimation-window sensitivity.
Result
Any portfolio of 3 to 10 stocks can be ranked on risk-adjusted return rather than return alone, and the weights behind each one are visible. Deployed and public, so you can test it on your own tickers.

Skills proved

  • Python
  • Streamlit
  • Portfolio theory
  • Git & GitHub
  • AI-assisted development

04

How I work

01

Write the brief before building

Before a model, a deck or a tool exists, I write down who it's for, what decision it has to support, and what it must never do. It's also how I direct AI tools — a written specification rather than a one-line request — and it's the difference between something that gets used and something that gets rebuilt.

02

Trace every number to a source

AI tools produce confident details that are wrong. I've caught them inventing a team on a project I did alone and a fact about me that was never true. Every figure on this site traces back to a document I can show you.

03

Explain it to someone who doesn't do it

Two years tutoring student-athletes in finance, accounting and statistics around their training schedules. If I can't explain a model in plain language, I don't understand it well enough to defend it.
